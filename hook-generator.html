How to use this tool

  1. Type your video topic — for example “growing a faceless YouTube channel”.
  2. Type your viewers’ pain point, such as “not getting views”.
  3. Click Generate to get five hook lines.
  4. Pick one, say it in your own words in the first 15 seconds, then deliver the payoff.

Worked example

Topic “growing a faceless YouTube channel”, pain point “not getting views”. Two of the five hooks:

  • “If you’re struggling with not getting views, this video will show you a simpler way to handle growing a faceless YouTube channel.”
  • “Most people fail at growing a faceless YouTube channel because they miss one small step. Here it is.”

The second works well as an opener: it names a mistake and promises the fix. Only use it if the video really shows that step.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Starting with a long intro about yourself. Viewers decide in seconds, so lead with the problem.
  • Promising a result the video never delivers. A strong hook with a weak video hurts retention.
  • Using a hook word for word without checking it fits. A vague pain point gives a vague hook, so be specific about the problem.

Frequently asked questions

What is a video hook?

A hook is the first 10 to 15 seconds of your video. Its job is to confirm the title promise and give viewers a reason to keep watching instead of clicking away.

Why do hooks matter for the algorithm?

YouTube promotes videos with strong audience retention. A weak opening makes viewers leave early, which signals the algorithm to recommend your video less.

Should my hook repeat the title?

Restate the promise in your own words rather than reading the title aloud. Then tease the payoff - what the viewer will learn or see - within the first 15 seconds.

How long should a hook be?

Aim for one or two sentences you can say in 10 to 15 seconds. If it takes longer, shorten it.

Should the hook be different from the title?

Yes. Restate the promise in fresh words and add a detail the title left out.
