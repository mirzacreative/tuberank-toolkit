How to use this tool

  1. Type your video topic — a specific phrase such as “YouTube SEO”, not a single word.
  2. Add who the video is for, for example “beginners”.
  3. Add a tone such as “curiosity” or “bold”, or leave it blank.
  4. Click Generate and copy the best fit. Shorten it past about 60 characters.

Worked example

Topic “YouTube SEO”, audience “beginners”, no tone. Ten titles, including:

  • I Tried YouTube SEO for 7 Days — Here’s What Happened
  • Ultimate YouTube SEO Guide for beginners
  • Stop Making These YouTube SEO Mistakes
  • I Tested 5 YouTube SEO Methods — This Won

Best pick: the first title. It promises a test and a result, and puts the keyword first.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using a vague topic such as “YouTube”. The titles come out generic.
  • Promising something the video never shows. Viewers leave early and trust drops.
  • Keeping a long title as it is. On a phone, titles cut off after about 60 characters.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a good YouTube title?

A good title is specific, honest and curiosity-driven. It includes your main keyword near the front, promises a clear benefit, and matches what the video actually delivers.

How long should a YouTube title be?

YouTube allows 100 characters, but aim for 50 to 60 characters so the full title shows on mobile and in search results without being cut off.

Should I use clickbait titles?

No. Misleading titles may earn a click but hurt watch time and trust, which damages rankings. Use curiosity, not deception.

Will this tool upload videos for me?

No. It only generates text suggestions - you copy the titles you like and paste them into YouTube Studio yourself.

Should I put my keyword at the start of the title?

Usually yes. Searchers scan the first few words, and YouTube weighs early words more. Keep the title natural — keyword first, benefit right after.

Can I use the same title as a bigger channel?

Copying a title rarely works: the other channel's audience and watch history carry it. Use the angle, not the exact words, and match it to your own video.
